<DESCRIPTION>
The primary issue is that val_slope collapses to 0.006 (8.2% of train_slope), indicating severe regime-specific overfitting. The model learns patterns from the training period that don't generalize to the validation period.

My strategy addresses this through three coordinated changes:

1. **Shorter lookback windows**: Replace long lookbacks (60, 200 bars) with shorter ones (5-20 bars). Shorter windows adapt faster to regime changes and are less likely to encode regime-specific patterns. For example, volatility measured over 20 bars is more regime-invariant than over 200 bars.

2. **Z-score normalization within rolling windows**: Instead of using raw values like `vol_20 / vol_200_mean`, I'll compute z-scores of features within rolling windows. This makes features regime-invariant - a "high volatility" reading becomes relative to recent context rather than absolute. For instance, `(vol_20 - vol_20.rolling(60).mean()) / vol_20.rolling(60).std()` normalizes volatility relative to its recent distribution.

3. **Rate-of-change features**: Add features that measure how market character is changing (volatility trend, momentum acceleration) rather than absolute levels. These capture regime transitions rather than regime states.

4. **Reduce model complexity**: Lower n_estimators from 400 to 250 and increase min_child_weight from 1 to 3. This forces the model to learn only the most robust, high-sample patterns that generalize across regimes.

5. **Feature interaction simplification**: Keep only the most generalizable interaction (momentum × volatility_regime) and remove complex multi-way interactions that likely overfit.

This approach should increase val_slope from 0.006 to >0.020 by making features more regime-invariant while maintaining or improving directional accuracy.
