<DESCRIPTION>
The current configuration uses learning_rate=0.045 with 55 early_stopping_rounds and 640 estimators, but the prediction collapse (pred_std_ratio=0.068) and low val_slope (0.0022) suggest the model is under-expressing the signal captured by the 80 features. The shallow tree depth (max_depth=3) combined with the low learning rate means each tree contributes very little to the final prediction, causing the model to converge toward the mean.

Looking at the historical data:
- Generation 5 (learning_rate=0.15, depth=5, no regularization) achieved pred_std_ratio=0.112 and val_slope=0.006 - better expression but overfit
- Generation 6 (learning_rate=0.055, depth=4) achieved pred_std_ratio=0.112 and val_slope=0.006 - the best balance so far
- Generations 4, 7, and current (depth=3) all show severe collapse with pred_std_ratio < 0.09

The key insight is that depth=3 is too shallow to capture the feature interactions needed for this problem. However, rather than just increasing depth (which failed in Gen 5 due to overfitting), I'll increase the learning rate to 0.07-0.08 while maintaining depth=4 and adjusting early stopping to 40-45 rounds. This allows:

1. Stronger individual tree contributions (higher learning rate)
2. Better feature interaction modeling (depth=4 vs depth=3)
3. Maintained generalization through early stopping
4. Similar total training budget (640 * 0.045 ≈ 560 * 0.055 ≈ 480 * 0.07)

This approach contrasts with the failed shallow-tree programs by maintaining sufficient model capacity while allowing more aggressive learning. The current configuration's 640 estimators with learning_rate=0.045 creates too many weak learners that can't escape the mean-reversion trap.
